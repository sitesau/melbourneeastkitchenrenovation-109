Kitchen Renovations Eastern Melbourne Local Options Compared Explained

Most eastern Melbourne kitchen projects fall into one of three scopes, and each carries its own cost drivers. A full rebuild strips the room back: demolition, rough-in for services, new cabinetry, benchtops and a staged handover. A makeover leaves the layout, service points and appliance positions alone and concentrates on doors, benchtops and splashbacks. Between the two sit custom jobs such as made-to-measure cabinetry or a butler's pantry, where storage and fit, rather than the whole room, drive the spend.

Comparing options fairly means comparing like with like. The published guidance notes that a kitchen budget is shaped less by colour scheme than by demolition, cabinetry, benchtop selection, plumbing, electrical work, splashback, appliances and finishing. When two proposals differ widely, the gap usually sits in those line items rather than in the look of the room, so ask each provider to spell them out before you judge value.

Comparing two quotes line by line

The published process opens with a free measure-up at your home, then works through layout, materials, lighting and drawings before an itemised fixed-price written quote is issued. Once you hold two or more quotes, read them as documents rather than prices. Each should state what is included, what is excluded, how variations are handled and which selections could move the total before anything is ordered.

Line items worth checking across quotes include cabinetry, benchtops, plumbing, electrical work, splashback and appliances, plus who coordinates the job when several trades are involved. Vague allowances and missing exclusions are classic warning signs at this stage, and they are examined in detail in a companion guide to red flags when planning eastern Melbourne kitchen renovations. Comparing quotes on identical scope is the only honest comparison.

Benchtop choices under the silica rules

Benchtop selection has become a compliance question as well as a style one. From 1 July 2024, engineered stone with 1% or more crystalline silica was banned for manufacture, supply, processing and installation across Australia under model WHS Regulations. Any quote still pricing traditional engineered stone should prompt a direct question about what material will actually be supplied.

The published alternatives include porcelain, sintered stone, natural granite or marble, solid timber, stainless steel, laminate, and low-silica engineered stone under 1% crystalline silica. Because the benchtop is one of the larger cost levers in a kitchen, comparing quotes means comparing the specified material, not just the finish shown in a sample. Compliance extends past the surface too: projects use registered plumbing and electrical trades where required, with written compliance paperwork supplied at handover.

Where the six to twelve weeks go

The published sequence runs free measure-up, design and quote, build and install, then handover. Of the 6 to 12 weeks a full renovation typically takes from contract to handover, roughly 4 to 6 weeks sit in cabinetry and stone fabrication before anyone is on site. The remaining 2 to 4 weeks cover installation, plumbing, electrical work, tiling and finishing.

That split explains why late changes cost time as well as money. Moving a sink, swapping a benchtop material or adding storage after drawings are approved can ripple through trades, fabrication and appliance fit. Before sign-off, ask for the final layout, materials, lighting plan, appliance positions, splashback treatment and handover paperwork in writing, so expectations are locked in before fabrication begins.

How long does a full kitchen renovation take in eastern Melbourne? The published timing is about 6 to 12 weeks from contract to handover, made up of roughly 4 to 6 weeks of cabinetry and stone fabrication followed by 2 to 4 weeks of on-site installation, plumbing, electrical and tiling work.

Is engineered stone still allowed for kitchen benchtops? Not the traditional kind. Listed alternatives include porcelain, sintered stone, granite or marble, solid timber, stainless steel, laminate and low-silica engineered stone.

Do I need a written contract for kitchen work in Victoria? Yes, for major domestic building work over $10,000 a written contract is required under the Domestic Building Contracts Act 1995.
